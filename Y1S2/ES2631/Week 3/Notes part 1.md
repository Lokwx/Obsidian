Quoting from sources
1) Use a lead-in clause/phrase to introduce the quote. Incorporate the quote into your sentence.
2) If the original quote is long, consider breaking it up using an interrupted structure
3) After inserting the quotation, provide a closing comment or explanation that either emphasizes the relevance of the quotation or helps advance the argument in some way.

Attribution structures
1) Integral - The cited author is named in the sentence.
	1) To emphasize the author or agent; useful for foregrounding your authorial voice when engaging with scholarship.
2) Non-integral - The author is referenced in parentheses (if using APA) or using superscript numbers.
	1) To emphasize the reported message or process over the author/writer.

Reporting Verbs
1) Verbs to convey Research Acts (findings and procedures)
	1) Findings – observe, discover, notice, show Procedures – analyze, calculate, measure, assay, explore
2) Verbs to convey Cognition Acts related to mental processes believe, conceptualize, suspect, view 
	1) believe, conceptualize, suspect, view
3) Verbs to convey Discourse Acts related to verbal expression ascribe, discuss, hypothesize, explain, state, remark, mention, articulate, convey
	1) ascribe, discuss, hypothesize, explain, state, remark, mention, articulate, convey
4) Evaluative verbs that attribute a position to the author being cited and sometimes communicating the writer’s stance or judgment on the cited source
	1) Reporting the information cited as true: acknowledge, point out, establish negative: fail, overlook, exaggerate, ignore positive: advocate, argue, hold, see neutral: address, cite, comment, examine tentative: allude to, hypothesize, believe, suggest critical: attack, condemn, object, refute
