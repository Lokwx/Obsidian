### The Consensus Theorem Statements

There are two dual forms of the Consensus Law:

1. **AND-OR Form:**

    $$

AB + \bar{A}C + BC = AB + \bar{A}C

$$
    
    _(The term $BC$ is redundant and can be removed.)_
    
2. **OR-AND Form (Dual):**
    
    $$
(A + B)(\bar{A} + C)(B + C) = (A + B)(\bar{A} + C)
$$

    _(The factor $(B + C)$ is redundant and can be removed.)_
    

### How to Identify the Consensus Term ($BC$)

To spot when the Consensus Law applies in an expression with three terms:

1. Look for a variable and its complement in two different terms (e.g., **$A$** in $AB$ and **$\bar{A}$** in $\bar{A}C$).
    
2. Look at the remaining variables in those two terms (**$B$** and **$C$**).
    
3. If there is a third term consisting of those two remaining variables combined together (**$BC$**), that third term is the **consensus term** and can be dropped completely.
